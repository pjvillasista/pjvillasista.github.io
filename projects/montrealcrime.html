Montreal Crime Analysis

Python SQL Tableau

An end-to-end analysis of Montreal's open crime dataset — data cleaning in Python, SQL-based pattern queries, and an interactive Tableau dashboard mapping crime hotspots and seasonal trends for city planning and law enforcement use.

The Challenge

Montreal's open crime data covers multiple years and crime categories, but in raw form it's not actionable — inconsistent formatting, missing values, and no geographic aggregation. The goal was to clean it and surface patterns that could inform where law enforcement should focus and how city planners should allocate resources.

The Approach

1. Data Collection: Downloaded raw crime incident data from Montreal's open data portal, covering multiple crime categories across all city neighborhoods.

2. Data Cleaning (Python): Standardized date formats, normalized category labels, resolved encoding issues in French-language fields, and dropped or imputed records with missing coordinates.

3. SQL Analysis: Queried the cleaned dataset to identify: crime frequency by neighborhood, hour-of-day distribution, seasonal trends, and year-over-year change by crime type.

4. Tableau Dashboard: Built a master dashboard with a geographic heatmap, time-series trend lines, and category breakdowns — filterable by year, crime type, and district.

The Outcome

An interactive dashboard that shows crime concentration by neighborhood, peaks by hour and season, and trend direction per category. Law enforcement can identify hotspot patterns; city planners can cross-reference with infrastructure decisions.

Technologies Used

  • Python — data cleaning and preprocessing
  • SQL — pattern analysis and aggregation queries
  • Tableau — geographic and time-series visualizations
